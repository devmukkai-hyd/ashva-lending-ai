# Sector Underwriting Note: Light Engineering

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 4.2: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING NOTE – LIGHT ENGINEERING SME LENDING**
**DOCUMENT ID: POL-SEC-LE-042**
**EFFECTIVE DATE: OCTOBER 1, 2023**
**AUTHORITY: CHIEF CREDIT OFFICER (ARJUN MEHTA)**

---

### 4.2.1 Purpose and Scope
This document sets forth the mandatory credit underwriting standards, risk parameters, and monitoring requirements for all commercial loan facilities extended to Small and Medium Enterprises operating within the Light Engineering sector. Underwriters must adhere strictly to these criteria. Any deviation requires prior written approval from the Credit Exception Committee, chaired by the Chief Risk Officer and Chief Credit Officer.

### 4.2.2 Sector Working Capital Cycle
1. The typical operating working capital cycle for Light Engineering SMEs—comprising raw material holding, precision machining/assembly, finished goods inventory, and receivable collection—is defined as **75 days**.
2. Underwriters must evaluate the applicant’s Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) against this baseline of 75 days using audited financials or the most recent 12-month trailing management accounts.
3. Any applicant exhibiting a working capital cycle exceeding 110 days must be subjected to a detailed aging analysis of receivables and inventory. Facilities for applicants with a cycle exceeding 120 days shall be declined outright unless secured by cash equivalents or unencumbered immovable property at a minimum 1.67x collateral coverage ratio.

### 4.2.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
1. Notwithstanding the general baseline DSCR floor of 1.25x stipulated in Section 2.1 of this manual, all Light Engineering SME loan applications must maintain a minimum projected and historical **DSCR floor of 1.35x**.
2. The DSCR calculation must incorporate projected principal and interest repayments over the facility tenor, tested under a base-case cash flow model. 
3. A sensitivity stress test must be applied assuming a 15% reduction in gross operating margins and a 30-day extension in average receivable collection periods; under this stress scenario, the resulting stressed DSCR must not fall below 1.10x.

### 4.2.4 Seasonality Adjustments and Working Capital Lines
1. Light Engineering SMEs frequently exhibit pronounced demand fluctuations linked to automotive, industrial machinery, and consumer durables manufacturing cycles.
2. The recognized **seasonal peak for the sector spans from January to March**, during which inventory build-up and receivable outstandings typically increase by 25% to 40% over baseline figures.
3. To accommodate this cycle, underwriters may approve a seasonal sub-limit under the revolving working capital facility, subject to the following mandatory controls:
   - The seasonal sub-limit shall not exceed 25% of the primary working capital limit.
   - The seasonal facility must be fully drawn down only between December 1 and February 15, and must be fully repaid to zero balance (clean-up period) for a minimum of 30 consecutive days between August 1 and October 31.
   - Drawing availability during the peak window must be governed by a monthly Borrowing Base Certificate submitted to the Operations Department no later than the 5th business day of each month.

### 4.2.5 Primary Causes of Sector Distress
Underwriters must specifically address and mitigate the two primary historical causes of default in the Light Engineering sector within the credit memo:
1. **Raw Material Price Volatility:** Unhedged exposure to primary metal inputs (e.g., electrolytic copper, cold-rolled steel coils, aluminum ingots). Underwriters must verify that the borrower maintains formula-pricing contracts with downstream buyers or holds adequate inventory buffer reserves to absorb input cost spikes exceeding 10% within a rolling 90-day window.
2. **Customer Concentration Risk:** Over-reliance on a single Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) or Tier-1 buyer. If the top customer accounts for more than **30%** of total annual revenues, the underwriting memo must include a detailed assessment of the buyer’s credit rating, historical payment track record, and the legal enforceability of the supply contract.

### 4.2.6 Acceptable Collateral Types and Valuation
Loan facilities to Light Engineering SMEs must be secured by a combination of the following acceptable collateral types, registered via the corporate registry:
1. **Primary Security:** First-ranking hypothecation over current assets, specifically raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods, and book debts, subject to a minimum margin of 25% on inventory and 30% on receivables aged under 90 days.
2. **Secondary Security:** First or second charge on plant, machinery, and industrial equipment, valued at Net Orderly Liquidation Value (NOLV) by an accredited panel valuer, subject to a maximum valuation age of 12 months.
3. **Collateral Margins:** The cumulative loan-to-value (LTV) ratio across all pledged assets shall not exceed 75% of the combined realizable value. Personal guarantees from all promoters holding greater than 20% equity stake are mandatory.

### 4.2.7 Portfolio Exposure Limits
1. To ensure adequate sector diversification within the Ashva Capital commercial loan book, the **maximum aggregate exposure to the Light Engineering sector is capped at 14.0 percent of the total loan book**.
2. As of the effective date of this policy, the current actual sector exposure stands at **11.5 percent** of the total loan book.
3. Underwriters are permitted to approve new facilities within this sector provided the incremental exposure does not breach the 14.0 percent ceiling. If portfolio monitoring reports indicate sector exposure has reached 13.5 percent, the Head of Portfolio Management shall automatically trigger a freeze on all new Light Engineering sector approvals, notifying the Credit Committee within 48 hours.

### 4.2.8 Tenor Limits
1. The **maximum tenor for term loan facilities extended to Light Engineering SMEs is 72 months**, inclusive of any permitted initial moratorium period.
2. Moratoriums on principal repayment shall not exceed 6 months from the date of initial disbursement, during which servicing of interest must remain current.
3. Working capital demand loans and revolving credit lines shall be sanctioned for a maximum validity period of 12 months, subject to annual review and renewal contingent upon clean compliance and recertified financial statements.
