# Sector Underwriting Note: Food Processing

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 8: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING MANDATE — FOOD PROCESSING SMES**
**Document Ref: CP-M-842-FP**
**Effective Date: 14 January 2025**

---

### 8.1 PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This document establishes the mandatory underwriting parameters, risk tolerances, and monitoring requirements for Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the Food Processing sector. All underwriters, credit committee members, and risk officers are strictly bound by the thresholds and directives contained herein. Deviations require approval pursuant to Section 8.6.

---

### 8.2 SECTOR WORKING CAPITAL CYCLE
8.2.1 The typical gross working capital cycle for SME food processors—encompassing raw material procurement, processing, packaging, and accounts receivable realization—is defined as **45 days**.

8.2.2 Underwriters shall evaluate current asset and current liability structures against the 45-day baseline. Any observed working capital elongation exceeding 15 days above baseline (i.e., aggregate cycle > 60 days) requires mandatory submission of a working capital remediation plan prior to facility sanction.

8.2.3 Inventory holding periods for perishable and semi-processed goods must not exceed 30 days of the total 45-day cycle without explicit sign-off from the Senior Sector Risk Manager, Marcus Vance.

---

### 8.3 DEBT SERVICE COVERAGE RATIO (DSCR) FLOOR
8.3.1 The minimum Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) floor for all Food Processing SME exposures is **1.25x**. 

8.3.2 The 1.25x DSCR floor applies across all projected operational scenarios, inclusive of standard base-case cash flows and light stress-testing (minimum 10% contraction in EBITDA margin).

8.3.3 No underwriting discretion is granted to approve facilities with a calculated DSCR below 1.25x for this sector.

---

### 8.4 SEASONALITY ADJUSTMENTS AND PEAK PERIODS
8.4.1 The designated seasonal peak period for the food processing sector runs from **October to January**, driven by agricultural harvest schedules and festive consumption surges.

8.4.2 During the seasonal peak window (October through January), temporary working capital overdraft limits or revolving credit lines may be drawn up to an additional **25%** above the approved baseline facility limit, provided historical cash conversion cycles support the incremental requirement.

8.4.3 Repayment schedules for seasonal term loans must account for cash flow troughs during the off-peak months (February to September). Underwriters must structure principal moratoriums or balloon payment adjustments if off-peak debt service coverage drops below 1.10x, provided the annual average maintains the 1.25x DSCR floor.

---

### 8.5 PRIMARY CAUSES OF SECTOR DISTRESS
Underwriters must explicitly address and mitigate the two most common causes of financial distress inherent to the food processing sector in their credit memo risk assessment:

1. **Input Price Volatility:** Unhedged spikes in raw agricultural commodity prices (e.g., grains, dairy, proteins) compressing gross margins before output prices can be adjusted. Underwriters must verify historical margin resilience and input cost pass-through mechanisms.
2. **Cold Chain Failure and Inventory Spoilage:** Unforeseen refrigeration breakdowns, logistical bottlenecks, or power grid failures resulting in mass inventory write-offs. Underwriters must inspect insurance coverage policies for consequential loss and spoilage riders.

---

### 8.6 COLLATERAL ELIGIBILITY AND VALUATION
8.6.1 Acceptable collateral types for Food Processing SME facilities are restricted to the following, subject to maximum advance rates:
* **First charge on Plant and Machinery (processing lines, packaging units):** Maximum advance rate of **60%** of Forced Sale Value (FSV).
* **First charge on Commercial Real Estate (processing plants, modern warehousing):** Maximum advance rate of **70%** of FSV.
* **Hypothecation of Book Debts and Inventory:** Maximum advance rate of **50%** of eligible receivables (<90 days) and non-perishable finished goods.

8.6.2 All collateral must be valued by an Ashva Capital-empanelled independent surveyor not more than 90 days prior to facility disbursement.

---

### 8.7 PORTFOLIO EXPOSURE CAPS AND TENOR LIMITS
8.7.1 The maximum aggregate exposure Ashva Capital shall hold in the Food Processing SME sector is **12 percent** of the total loan book. 

8.7.2 As of the effective date of this manual, current portfolio exposure stands at **9.8 percent** of the loan book, leaving a residual sector headroom of **2.2 percent**. Underwriters shall verify real-time exposure availability via the Credit Risk Dashboard prior to issuing any conditional sanction letter.

8.7.3 The maximum facility tenor permitted for any term loan within this sector is **60 months**. Revolving credit facilities are subject to annual review and mandatory clean-down of at least 15 consecutive days within every 12-month cycle.

---

### 8.8 MANDATED NUMERIC THRESHOLDS SUMMARY
For ease of system validation, the core automated underwriting rules enforced by the Ashva Capital credit engine for this sector are consolidated below:

* **DSCR Floor:** 1.25x (Section 8.3.1)
* **Typical Working Capital Cycle:** 45 days (Section 8.2.1)
* **Maximum Sector Exposure:** 12% of loan book, currently at 9.8% (Section 8.7.1 & 8.7.2)
* **Maximum Facility Tenor:** 60 months (Section 8.7.3)
* **Seasonal Peak Window:** October to January (Section 8.4.1)
