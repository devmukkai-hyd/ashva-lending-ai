# Sector Underwriting Note: Auto Components

**ASHVA CAPITAL — INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 14: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING — AUTO COMPONENTS SME**

---

### 14.1 PURPOSE AND APPLICABILITY
14.1.1 This section governs all credit facilities extended to Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the auto components manufacturing and tier-2/tier-3 supply chain sector. 
14.1.2 Underwriters are strictly bound by the numeric thresholds, verification protocols, and structural covenants defined herein. Deviations require written sign-off from the Credit Committee chaired by the Chief Credit Officer, Marcus Vance.

### 14.2 SECTOR-SPECIFIC RISK PARAMETERS AND THRESHOLDS
14.2.1 **Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR):** Due to high capital asset depreciation and volatile raw material pricing, the minimum required DSCR for this sector is **1.35x**, superseding the baseline portfolio standard of 1.25x.
14.2.2 **Working Capital Cycle:** The typical working capital cycle for auto component SME borrowers is **80 days**, calculated from raw material inventory receipt to cash collection from Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) or tier-1 vendors. 
14.2.3 **Maximum Tenor:** Term loan facilities extended under this sector policy shall not exceed a maximum tenor of **60 months**.
14.2.4 **Portfolio Concentration Limit:** Aggregate exposure to the auto components SME sector shall not exceed **13.0 percent** of the total Ashva Capital loan book. As of the current reporting cycle, sector exposure stands at **12.1 percent**, leaving an available headroom of 0.9 percent for new originations.

### 14.3 SEASONALITY AND CASH FLOW ADJUSTMENTS
14.3.1 Auto component manufacturers experience a predictable seasonal peak in production and dispatch from **July to September**, driven by pre-festive inventory build-up across domestic automotive markets.
14.3.2 During the seasonal peak window (July to September), working capital revolving limits may be drawn up to 100 percent of the sanctioned drawing power, provided inventory aging reports confirm finished goods turn within 45 days.
14.3.3 For the trough period (April to May), underwriters must apply a 15 percent haircut to projected monthly cash inflows when calculating forward-looking DSCR to account for scheduled OEM plant shutdowns for annual maintenance.

### 14.4 PRIMARY CAUSES OF SECTOR DISTRESS
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and document mitigants for the two primary historical causes of default in the Ashva Capital auto components portfolio:
1. **OEM Concentration Risk:** Reliance on a single primary OEM customer for more than 40 percent of total annual revenue. *Mitigant:* Mandatory requirement that no single OEM customer accounts for >50 percent of top-line revenue, verified via GSTIN-mapped sales ledger audits.
2. **Raw Material Price Volatility:** Unhedged exposure to primary commodity price fluctuations (specifically aluminum, cold-rolled steel, and engineering polymers) that compress gross margins before price-escalation clauses with OEMs can be triggered. *Mitigant:* Borrower must demonstrate a minimum historical Gross Margin of 18 percent and a pass-through pricing mechanism embedded in customer supply contracts.

### 14.5 ACCEPTABLE COLLATERAL AND MARGIN REQUIREMENTS
14.5.1 Facilities must be secured by a composite security package comprising:
* **Primary Security:** Hypothecation of plant, machinery, and current assets (inventories and book debts under 90 days), subject to a minimum margin of 25 percent on machinery and 30 percent on book debts.
* **Collateral Security:** Equitable mortgage on industrial land and factory buildings owned by the operating entity or promoter, providing a minimum collateral coverage ratio of 1.25x against the total facility value.
14.5.2 Second-charge security over core operating assets is strictly prohibited unless an inter-creditor agreement with the senior institutional lender is executed and registered with the Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest (CERSAI) within 30 days of disbursement.
