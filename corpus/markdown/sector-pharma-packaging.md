# Sector Underwriting Note: Pharma Packaging

**ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 14: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING PROTOCOLS**
**SUB-SECTION 14.4: PHARMA PACKAGING SME LENDING**

---

### 1. Purpose and Scope
This sub-section establishes the mandatory credit parameters, risk thresholds, and collateral requirements for all small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the pharmaceutical packaging sector. Underwriters must adhere strictly to these stipulations. Deviations require explicit sign-off from the Credit Committee chaired by Chief Credit Officer Marcus Vance.

### 2. Working Capital Cycle Dynamics
2.1. The typical working capital cycle for approved entities in the pharma packaging sector is **60 days**, calculated from raw material inventory receipt (e.g., medical-grade polymers, aluminum foil, blister PVC) to cash collection from finished goods delivery to tier-1 and tier-2 pharmaceutical manufacturers.
2.2. Any borrower exhibiting a reported working capital cycle exceeding **95 days** shall trigger an mandatory operational audit by the Ashva Capital Risk Operations team prior to facility disbursement.

### 3. Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
3.1. Notwithstanding the standard baseline DSCR floor of 1.25x stipulated in Section 4.2 of this manual, all credit facilities extended to the pharma packaging sector are subject to a sector-specific DSCR floor of **1.30x**.
3.2. Projections must incorporate conservative pricing assumptions reflecting raw material price volatility in petrochemical inputs.

### 4. Seasonality Adjustments
4.1. Borrowers in this sector exhibit **no marked season**. Demand remains relatively flat across all four fiscal quarters due to non-discretionary pharmaceutical replenishment cycles.
4.2. Consequently, underwriters shall not apply seasonal revenue uplifts or working capital line expansions based on calendar quarters. Drawdown requests showing marked spikes must be justified by verified, executed purchase orders from accredited pharmaceutical manufacturers.

### 5. Primary Causes of Financial Distress
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and document mitigants against the two most common causes of distress in the pharma packaging sector:
5.1. **Regulatory and Compliance Failure:** Loss of ISO 15378 certification, failure of sterility audits, or non-compliance with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards imposed by regulatory authorities, leading to immediate cancellation of client master service agreements.
5.2. **Raw Material Price Concentration and Margin Squeeze:** Inability to pass through sudden cost increases in specialized polymers and cold-form aluminum foils to pharmaceutical clients tied under rigid, long-term fixed-price supply contracts.

### 6. Acceptable Collateral Types
The following collateral hierarchy is mandatory for all exposures in this sector:
6.1. **Primary:** First-ranking hypothecation over current assets, specifically book debts and inventory (raw materials, work-in-progress, and finished goods), subject to a minimum asset coverage ratio of **1.25:1**.
6.2. **Secondary:** First or second charge on plant and machinery (specifically blister-forming lines, extrusion machinery, and printing presses), valued at liquidation value net of a **35.0%** depreciation haircut.
6.3. **Personal Guarantees:** Unconditional and irrevocable personal guarantees from all promoters holding greater than **20.0%** equity stake in the borrowing entity.

### 7. Portfolio Exposure Caps and Tenor Limits
7.1. The maximum aggregate exposure Ashva Capital shall hold in the pharma packaging sector is **8.0%** of the total active loan book. As of the current reporting cycle, existing sector exposure stands at **6.4%**, leaving a remaining allocation headroom of **1.6%**. Underwriters must verify available headroom in the Loan Management System (LMS-4) prior to issuing any Sanction Letter.
7.2. The maximum allowable loan tenor for term facilities in this sector is **60 months**. Working capital demand loans shall be subject to an annual review with a maximum renewal cycle capped at the same **60-month** structural horizon.
