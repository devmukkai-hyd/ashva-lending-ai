# Sector Underwriting Note: Textiles

**ASHVA CAPITAL — INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 4: SECTOR-SPECIFIC UNDERWRITING MANDATES**
**SUB-SECTION 4.8: TEXTILE MANUFACTURING AND WHOLESALE SME LENDING**

### 4.8.1 Purpose and Scope
This policy governs the credit evaluation, risk grading, and exposure limits for all Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the textile sector (Standard Industrial Classification codes 1300 through 1350). Underwriters must strictly adhere to the numerical thresholds and operational restrictions contained herein. Deviation from these parameters requires written sign-off from Chief Risk Officer Marcus Vance.

### 4.8.2 Working Capital Cycle and Cash Flow Mechanics
(a) The typical working capital cycle for approved textile SMEs is 90 days, calculated from raw material procurement (yarn, grey fabric) to final cash collection from finished goods distribution.
(b) Underwriters must model cash flow projections based on a 90-day cash conversion cycle. Any applicant exhibiting an actual or projected operating cycle exceeding 115 days shall be subject to mandatory enhanced working capital monitoring under Section 4.8.2(c).
(c) Seasonal peak demand occurs strictly between August and October annually, driven by the pre-holiday retail inventory build. Working capital credit facilities must be structured to accommodate a 35% temporary line increase during this window, provided the borrower maintains a minimum unencumbered liquidity buffer of 1.5x the monthly debt service obligation.

### 4.8.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
(a) Notwithstanding the standard Ashva Capital commercial SME DSCR floor of 1.25x, the sector-specific DSCR floor for all textile sector exposures is 1.30x. 
(b) The 1.30x DSCR must be demonstrated on a historical 12-month trailing basis and projected forward across the entire requested tenor based on conservative stress-tested revenue assumptions (minimum 10% top-line contraction).

### 4.8.4 Maximum Exposure and Portfolio Concentration
(a) Ashva Capital shall maintain a maximum aggregate exposure to the textile sector not to exceed 15 percent of the total active loan book.
(b) As of the current reporting period, sector concentration stands at 13.2 percent of the loan book, leaving a residual aggregate capacity of 1.8 percent for new originations. Underwriters must verify current portfolio capacity with Portfolio Manager Sarah Jenkins prior to issuing any formal term sheet.

### 4.8.5 Loan Tenor and Structure Limits
(a) The maximum allowable tenor for any term loan or amortizing facility extended to a textile SME is 60 months. 
(b) Bullet repayments are strictly prohibited; all facilities must feature equal monthly principal amortizations following an optional moratorium period not to exceed 90 days.

### 4.8.6 Primary Causes of Sector Distress
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and document mitigants against the two most common causes of distress in the textile SME sector:
1. **Raw Material Price Volatility:** Unhedged fluctuations in global cotton and synthetic fiber input costs compressing gross margins by more than 400 basis points within a single quarter. Mitigant: Mandatory review of fixed-price supplier contracts covering a minimum of 60% of projected annual input requirements.
2. **Buyer Concentration and Default:** Over-reliance on three or fewer domestic or international apparel brands for greater than 50% of annual revenue, exposing the borrower to extended accounts receivable aging (>120 days) upon retail downturns. Mitigant: Verification of credit insurance or factoring agreements covering at least 70% of the top five buyer accounts.

### 4.8.7 Acceptable Collateral and Valuation Margins
Underwriters shall only accept the following collateral types, subject to the specified maximum Advance Rates against forced liquidation value (FLV):
1. **First-Priority General Security Agreement (GSA)** over accounts receivable and inventory: Maximum advance rate of 75% on eligible accounts receivable (aged under 90 days) and 50% on raw material/finished goods inventory.
2. **Commercial Real Estate (CRE):** First-ranking legal mortgage over owner-occupied industrial factory premises used directly in textile manufacturing: Maximum LTV of 65% based on an independent appraisal conducted within the preceding 180 days by a certified member of the Ashva Capital Valuations Panel.
