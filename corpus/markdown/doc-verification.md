# Document Verification and Fraud Screening Procedure

**ASHVA CAPITAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL**
**SECTION 4: DOCUMENT VERIFICATION AND FRAUD DETECTION PROCEDURES**
**DOCUMENT ID: CPM-SEC-4-V2.4**
**EFFECTIVE DATE: 14 OCTOBER 2023**

---

### 4.1 Purpose and Scope
This section establishes the mandatory standards, verification methodologies, tolerance thresholds, and escalation pathways for all SME credit applications processed by Ashva Capital. Compliance with this manual is mandatory for all underwriting personnel. Deviations from these procedures constitute a breach of credit policy and will result in internal audit review.

---

### 4.2 Mandatory Document Set for SME Applications
Underwriters must collect, ingest, and index the complete mandatory document set prior to initiating verification. Incomplete files shall not proceed to underwriting review.

1. **KYC and Entity Proofs:**
   * Certificate of Incorporation or Partnership Deed.
   * Permanent Account Number (PAN) card of the entity and all directors/partners holding greater than 20% equity.
   * Proof of principal place of business (Utility bill not older than 60 days or registered Lease Agreement).
2. **Financial Records:**
   * Audited financial statements (Balance Sheet, Profit and Loss Statement, Cash Flow Statement) for the preceding two (2) financial years, bearing the UDIN (Unique Document Identification Number) issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants.
   * Provisional financial statements for the current financial year-to-date, signed by two directors or partners.
3. **Tax Filings:**
   * Goods and Services Tax (GST) returns (GSTR-3B and GSTR-1) for the preceding twelve (12) consecutive months.
   * Income Tax Returns (ITR) of the entity for the preceding two (2) assessment years along with corresponding tax computation sheets and acknowledgment receipts.
4. **Banking Records:**
   * Electronic bank statements in native PDF or XML format for all primary and secondary current accounts declared by the applicant, covering the preceding twelve (12) consecutive months. Scanned paper statements are strictly prohibited.

---

### 4.3 Verification Steps by Document Type

#### 4.3.1 Bank Statements
1. **Integrity and Format Check:** Underwriters must verify that submitted PDF statements are digitally generated by the issuing bank and contain no metadata alterations, layer insertions, or font inconsistencies.
2. **Transaction Analysis:** Run automated transaction parsing via the Ashva Credit Engine (ACE) to verify:
   * Regular inward remittances matching declared business turnover.
   * Bounce charges, average monthly balance (AMB) maintenance, and instances of overdraft utilization.
   * End-of-day balances for the preceding 365 days.
3. **Source Cross-Referencing:** Inward credit descriptions must be cross-verified against major customer names declared in the trade reference sheet.

#### 4.3.2 GST Returns (GSTR-3B and GSTR-1)
1. **JSON Validation:** Underwriters must ingest the original JSON files downloaded directly from the GST portal by the applicant, or pull data via the authorized GST Suvidha Provider (GSP) API integration.
2. **Reconciliation:** Compare taxable turnover reported in GSTR-3B against outward supplies reported in GSTR-1 for each corresponding tax period.
3. **Filing Timelines:** Verify that filings were executed within the statutory due dates. Any filing delay exceeding fifteen (15) days post-deadline must be documented in the credit memo.

#### 4.3.3 Audited Financials
1. **UDIN Verification:** Underwriter must validate the printed UDIN on the audit report directly via the ICAI portal to confirm authenticity and matching signatory details.
2. **Schedule Cross-Checks:** Reconcile figures in the Profit and Loss statement against Schedules for Revenue, Cost of Goods Sold, and Operating Expenses.
3. **Contingent Liability Review:** Examine notes to accounts for unprovided liabilities, pending litigation, or corporate guarantees extended to third parties.

---

### 4.4 Revenue Discrepancy Tolerances and Thresholds
Underwriters shall compare the annual gross revenue declared in the application form against the verified revenue derived from audited financials, GST filings, and banking inflows.

1. **Acceptable Variance:** Declared revenue within **10 percent** of verified revenue is accepted without penalty.
2. **Moderate Variance (Reason Code `DOC_MISMATCH`):** A difference larger than 10 percent carries reason code `DOC_MISMATCH` and requires the application to be routed to a human senior credit reviewer for mandatory clarification from the applicant.
3. **Severe Variance (Reason Code `FRAUD_HOLD`):** A difference above **25 percent** carries reason code `FRAUD_HOLD` and immediately triggers a system-enforced fraud hold, locking the file from further underwriting progression.

---

### 4.5 Fraud Red Flag Triggers
The presence of any of the following indicators shall immediately assign reason code `FRAUD_HOLD` and route the file to the Risk and Fraud Operations Unit managed by Head of Risk, Marcus Vance.

1. **Metadata Anomalies:** Creation or modification dates on PDF bank statements or balance sheets falling after the application submission date, or showing software edits (e.g., Adobe Illustrator, Photoshop).
2. **Round-Tripping Indicators:** Multiple matching inward and outward wire transfers between the applicant's primary current account and connected entities within a 48-hour window, constituting more than 15% of reported monthly turnover.
3. **GST-Banking Mismatch:** Cumulative bank credits exceeding declared GSTR-3B taxable turnover by more than 40% without a valid, verifiable explanation regarding non-GST revenue streams (e.g., export income, interest income).
4. **Director Discrepancies:** Active DIN (Director Identification Number) status flagged for disqualification under Section 164 of the Companies Act, or matching entries on the Central Economic Intelligence Bureau (CEIB) negative database.
5. **Address Red Flags:** Registered business address matching known commercial mail-forwarding agencies, residential properties without commercial zoning permits, or matching addresses of known shell companies flagged in the Ashva Capital Risk Repository.

### 4.6 Escalation Path
All exceptions, discrepancies, and fraud holds must follow the strict hierarchical escalation path detailed below:

```
[Level 1: Underwriter] 
   │
   ├─► Declared vs. Verified Revenue > 10% ──► Assign `DOC_MISMATCH` ──► [Level 2: Senior Reviewer (Arjun Patel)]
   │
   └─► Any Fraud Trigger / Variance > 25% ──► Assign `FRAUD_HOLD` ──► [Level 3: Head of Risk (Marcus Vance)]
                                                                           │
                                                                           ▼
                                                             [Level 4: Credit Committee]
                                                           (Final Sign-off / Police Report)
```

1. **Level 1 (Underwriter):** Performs initial document intake and automated ACE verification. Has no authority to override variance thresholds or fraud holds.
2. **Level 2 (Senior Credit Reviewer - Arjun Patel):** Reviews files bearing code `DOC_MISMATCH`. Has authority to request supplemental documentation (e.g., debtor aging reports, CA certificate) and clear discrepancies if justified by verified secondary sources. Response SLA: 24 hours.
3. **Level 3 (Head of Risk - Marcus Vance):** Reviews files bearing code `FRAUD_HOLD`. Conducts independent investigation, interviews applicant management, and decides whether to release the hold or reject the application outright. Response SLA: 48 hours.
4. **Level 4 (Credit Committee):** Comprising Chief Credit Officer (Sarah Jenkins), Head of Risk (Marcus Vance), and Chief Executive Officer (David Thorne). Required for any exception override involving files previously flagged for severe fraud indicators. A unanimous vote of all three members is required to override a Level 3 `FRAUD_HOLD`.
