# Sector Underwriting Note: Logistics

# ASHVA CAPITAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL
## SECTION 4.12: SECTOR UNDERWRITING NOTE — LOGISTICS SME LENDING
**Document ID:** CP-LOG-2024-V4  
**Effective Date:** 1 January 2025  
**Owner:** Office of the Chief Credit Officer  
**Applicability:** Mandatory for all Ashva Capital Underwriters and Automated Decision Systems  

---

### 1.0 PURPOSE AND SECTOR APPLICABILITY
1.1 This note governs the credit evaluation, risk grading, and exposure management for small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the Logistics and Commercial Transport sector (SIC codes 4841, 4921).  
1.2 Underwriters are strictly bound by the numeric thresholds and qualitative criteria detailed herein. Where this sector note conflicts with general commercial lending guidelines, this note shall prevail.

### 2.0 PORTFOLIO CONCENTRATION AND CAPACITY LIMITS
2.1 The maximum aggregate credit exposure Ashva Capital shall hold in the Logistics sector is **10 percent** of the total active loan book.  
2.2 The portfolio is currently at its authorized cap of **10 percent**. Consequently, all new loan applications originating from the Logistics sector shall automatically be assigned reason code **SECTOR_CAP** by the underwriting engine.  
2.3 Automated approval pathways are disabled for this sector. Every application bearing reason code **SECTOR_CAP** must be routed directly to a human Senior Credit Reviewer for manual exception evaluation and portfolio substitution analysis.

### 3.0 WORKING CAPITAL CYCLE AND CASH FLOW DYNAMICS
3.1 The typical working capital cycle for prime Logistics SME borrowers is **30 days**, measured from invoice generation for freight and fulfillment services to cash settlement by shippers or enterprise consignors.  
3.2 Underwriters must adjust cash flow projections to account for structural debtor delays. Any borrower exhibiting a Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) exceeding 55 days shall not be eligible for standard working capital facilities without a mandatory collateral margin increase of 150 basis points.

### 4.0 DEBT SERVICE COVERAGE RATIO (DSCR) FLOOR
4.1 Notwithstanding the general commercial lending DSCR standard of 1.25x, the sector-specific DSCR floor for Logistics SME lending is **1.40x**.  
4.2 Projections must incorporate normalized historical earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA), adjusted for documented fleet maintenance capital expenditure reserves of no less than $0.05 per operational vehicle-mile.

### 5.0 SEASONALITY ADJUSTMENTS
6.1 The Logistics sector experiences a pronounced seasonal volume peak spanning **September to December** each year, driven by fourth-quarter retail inventory staging and peak-season parcel delivery surges.  
6.2 Underwriters must utilize annualized trailing twelve-month (TTM) cash flow statements rather than unadjusted peak-quarter figures. When evaluating debt service capacity during off-peak months (January to April), underwriters must verify that the borrower maintains a minimum liquid cash reserve equivalent to 60 days of fixed operating overhead.

### 6.0 MAXIMUM TENOR AND FACILITY STRUCTURES
6.1 The maximum allowable loan tenor for any term facility secured under this sector note is **48 months**.  
6.2 Revolving lines of credit shall be subject to an annual review deadline exactly 365 days from initial disbursement, with a mandatory 15% clean-down requirement for a period of 14 consecutive days during the off-peak window.

### 7.0 PRIMARY CAUSES OF SECTOR DISTRESS
7.1 Underwriters must explicitly stress-test applications against the two most common empirical causes of distress in the Logistics sector:
1. **Fuel Price Volatility:** Unhedged spikes in diesel or alternative fuel costs that outpace contract rate escalator clauses, eroding gross operating margins.
2. **Fleet Obsolescence and Maintenance Shock:** Unanticipated major mechanical failures (e.g., engine overhauls, transmission replacements) combined with regulatory non-compliance fines for aging vehicle cohorts.

### 8.0 ACCEPTABLE COLLATERAL TYPES
8.1 Eligible collateral is strictly limited to the following asset classes, subject to the indicated maximum advance rates:
1. **Commercial Fleet Vehicles:** Heavy-duty trucks, refrigerated trailers, and delivery vans (Model year not exceeding 6 years at origination), valued at 65% of Net Orderly Liquidation Value (NOLV).
2. **Logistics Warehousing Equipment:** Automated sorting machinery, pallet racking, and forklift fleets, valued at 50% of NOLV.
3. **Accounts Receivable:** Rated commercial freight receivables aged under 45 days, valued at 75% of face value, subject to daily verification under a controlled lockbox arrangement.

### 9.0 GOVERNANCE AND EXCEPTIONS
9.1 Any deviation from the parameters established in this document—including overrides of reason code **SECTOR_CAP**—requires joint written authorization from the Head of Credit Risk and the Chief Credit Officer, accompanied by a completed Risk Mitigation Memorandum filed in the central credit archive within 24 hours of approval.
