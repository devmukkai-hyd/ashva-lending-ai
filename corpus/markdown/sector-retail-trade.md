# Sector Underwriting Note: Retail Trade

# ASHVA CAPITAL INTERNAL CREDIT POLICY MANUAL
## SECTION 4.12: SECTOR UNDERWRITING NOTE — RETAIL TRADE SME LENDING

### 4.12.1 Purpose and Scope
This policy note establishes the mandatory underwriting parameters for Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) borrowers operating within the Retail Trade sector. All credit assessments, risk tiering, and limit approvals must comply with the numeric thresholds and structural rules set forth herein. Deviation from these parameters requires unanimous approval from the Credit Committee, chaired by the Chief Risk Officer and Chief Credit Officer.

### 4.12.2 Working Capital Cycle and Cash Flow Dynamics
(a) The typical working capital cycle for approved Retail Trade SME borrowers is established at **40 days**, calculated from the inventory purchase date through accounts receivable collection to supplier settlement. 
(b) Underwriters must verify that the applicant’s historical cash conversion cycle does not exceed **55 days**. Any cycle exceeding 55 days requires a mandatory deduction of 15% from the qualifying borrowing base.
(c) Operating cash flow projections must incorporate historical inventory holding periods derived from the past three years of audited or reviewed financial statements.

### 4.12.3 Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Floor
(a) The sector-specific DSCR floor for Retail Trade SME lending is **1.25x**, aligning with Ashva Capital's baseline standard. 
(b) No exceptions or downward adjustments to this **1.25x** floor are permitted for standard retail credits, regardless of collateral coverage or guarantor net worth.

### 4.12.4 Seasonality Adjustments and Peak Working Capital
(a) Retail Trade borrowers experience a pronounced seasonal peak occurring annually from **October to December**.
(b) During this seasonal peak (**October to December**), underwriters may approve a temporary revolving credit line increase of up to **25%** above the approved base limit, provided the borrower demonstrates a minimum unencumbered cash buffer equal to **10%** of the requested seasonal advance.
(c) Repayment of the seasonal facility tranche must be fully amortized or swept from peak-period cash receipts no later than **February 28** of the following calendar year.

### 4.12.5 Common Causes of Financial Distress
Underwriters must explicitly evaluate and document mitigants for the two primary drivers of default in the Retail Trade sector:
1. **Unmanaged Inventory Obsolescence:** Accumulation of slow-moving or seasonal stock that fails to liquidate, leading to severe liquidity contraction. Mitigant: Mandatory requirement for monthly perpetual inventory reports and aging schedules for inventory older than **90 days**.
2. **Margin Compression from Discounter Aggression:** Unanticipated contraction in gross margins driven by localized or online price competition without corresponding volume growth. Mitigant: Minimum baseline gross margin threshold of **28%** required at underwriting.

### 4.12.6 Acceptable Collateral Types and Valuation Haircuts
Collateral security for Retail Trade SME facilities is restricted to the following eligible asset classes, subject to the specified advance rates:
1. **First-Ranking General Security Agreement (GSA) / Floating Charge over Inventory:** Maximum advance rate of **50%** of the net orderly liquidation value (NOLV), excluding perishable goods and private-label stock older than **180 days**.
2. **Commercial Real Estate (if owner-occupied):** Maximum LTV of **65%** based on an independent appraisal dated within the preceding **12 months**.
3. **Director’s Personal Guarantee:** Joint and several guarantees from all shareholders holding greater than **20%** equity interest, backed by a statement of net worth verified by Ashva Capital's internal collateral review team.

### 4.12.7 Portfolio Concentration Limits and Tenor Caps
(a) The maximum aggregate credit exposure Ashva Capital will hold in the Retail Trade sector is **12 percent of the total loan book**. As of the current reporting cycle, sector exposure stands at **8.9 percent**, leaving an available capacity of **3.1 percent**.
(b) The maximum allowable loan tenor for any facility under this sector policy is **36 months**. Bullet repayment structures are prohibited; all facilities must feature equal monthly principal and interest amortization.
